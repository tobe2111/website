Results from an overseas trade show are decided by the meetings you book before the show and the follow-up in the two weeks after it, not by booth design. The few days on site only produce as much as you prepared for. Try moving half the time you planned to spend on booth decoration to your buyer list and follow-up emails.

Key takeaways

  • Before the show, decide which buyers to meet and book the meetings in advance.
  • On site, record each conversation in the same format instead of collecting business cards.
  • After the show, the company that sends the promised materials first is the one that is remembered.
  • Set the goal as the number of quote requests and samples sent, not the number of meetings.

Why does preparation decide results more than the booth?

Buyers also arrive with a schedule. They decide in advance which companies to meet and walk the hall in the time left over. Many people pass the booth, but only some of them can make purchasing decisions, and most of those people's time is already filled before the show.

A buyer you contacted and booked in advance is different. They have already looked at your product materials once, so the first question goes straight to price and quantity. This is where the difference arises between a 30-minute meeting that ends with a company introduction and one that gets as far as quotation terms.

Do not write a long company introduction in the invitation email. Write one point where your product meets the range that buyer handles, your booth number and two or three available times.

In Korea, part of the participation fee and booth cost can sometimes be covered by national or local government support programs. Support from KOTRA (the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency) comes in two broad forms.

  • Group participation in the Korea Pavilion. You exhibit in one booth of the Korea Pavilion at a show the government has selected for support that year. You receive support for part of the booth rental and construction costs and for one-way shipping, and because the official forwarder and booth contractor are already appointed, the preparation burden is lighter. A Korea Pavilion directory and a list of potential buyers are also provided. The order is to apply to the call for participants, pay the application deposit and be selected after screening. Recruitment starts several months before the show opens.
  • Individual participation support. This is for exhibiting directly with a stand-alone booth under your own name at a show that is not on the supported list. You pay the organizer first and take part, then submit proof and get back part of your actual costs within a limit. This means you have to fund the whole participation fee up front.

If the cost of the same show is also supported by a program of another ministry or a local government, you are excluded, and sharing a booth with another company also rules out individual participation support. Support rates, limits and recruitment schedules change every year, so look up the announcement as soon as you decide to take part. What should you prepare and record on site?

Price list and samples

If you answer a price question with "we will send it after we get back", the conversation ends there. At a minimum, you should be able to answer these four points on the spot.

  • MOQ (minimum order quantity) and unit prices by quantity tier
  • The trade term the unit price is based on. The figure changes depending on how far freight is included in the price
  • The time production and shipment take
  • Whether you hold the certifications that country requires and, if not, by when you can have them

If you are not comfortable disclosing exact unit prices, at least prepare a range for each quantity tier. For how the unit price differs by trade term, see Export quote basics: how EXW, FOB, CIF and DDP differ.

Samples and exhibits may go through customs differently from commercial cargo, and some product types, such as food and cosmetics, are restricted from being brought in. This part is general information. Confirm how it applies to your case with the relevant authorities or a professional.

Meeting records

If you come back with only business cards, a week later you will not remember who asked what. Write each meeting down in the same format as soon as it ends. Company and job title, the product they showed interest in, the questions they asked, their current supplier and what you promised to send are enough. Photograph the business card together with the record sheet so the pair stays matched.

That evening, grade each meeting. Three groups are enough: those who named a specific quantity and timing, those who are interested but have not settled terms, and those who were only gathering information. This grade decides whom you contact first after you return home.

What should you do in the two weeks after the show?

  1. Send the first email within three days. Do not write only a thank-you. Include one line about what you discussed on site and the materials you promised.
  2. Send a quote to buyers who named a quantity and timing. Give those who asked for samples the shipping schedule and the tracking number.
  3. A week later, contact those who have not replied once more. Instead of "did you see my email", attach one new piece of information, such as a copy of a certificate or the unit price for a different pack size.
  4. Propose a video meeting to buyers whose terms are not settled.
  5. Keep the results in a table. If you write down the number of meetings, quote requests and samples sent, and the cost, you can judge whether to exhibit at the next show.

Common mistakes

  • Spending most of the budget on booth decoration and no time on advance contact.
  • Sending only one person, and missing other buyers who come by during a meeting.
  • Leaving product explanations to a local interpreter without briefing them in advance.
  • Reporting the number of business cards collected as the result.
  • Sending the same follow-up email to every buyer in one batch.
  • Dealing first with the work that piled up at home during the show, so the follow-up slips to a month later.
