With online marketplaces in Southeast Asia, you can only choose after deciding which country you will sell in and how. The platform name comes second. Products that customers search for tend to suit Shopee or Lazada, and products that sell only when shown on video tend to suit TikTok Shop. If this is your first time, start with one country and one platform.

Key takeaways

  • Southeast Asia is not one market. Language, payment, logistics and regulations differ by country.
  • Shopee and Lazada run on search and sales events. TikTok Shop runs on video and live streams.
  • The permits you need depend on whether you ship directly from Korea or hold stock locally.
  • Fees and listing terms can change, so check each platform's latest guidance before you proceed.

The deciding factor is the nature of the product. Products that are already known by name, or that customers search for by category name, fit search-based marketplaces. Products that people only want after seeing them in use, such as color cosmetics, snacks and small household goods, are sold by video. Why decide on the country first?

Because the same platform behaves like a different app in each country. Product listings, language, event calendars and logistics are split by country. The countries each platform operates in also differ. Look at these four points when you choose a country.

  • Language: in some countries English is enough, and in others product pages and customer service in the local language are a must.
  • Payment: e-wallets have grown quickly, but cash on delivery remains, and in some countries, such as the Philippines, it is used more outside the big cities. Where many orders are placed this way, refused deliveries and returns to sender become a cost.
  • Logistics: in countries with many islands, delivery time and cost vary widely by region.
  • Regulations: for cosmetics, each country has a procedure for notifying the authorities before sale, and food requires registration or a check against ingredient standards. In countries with large Muslim populations, halal certification (confirmation that a product conforms to Islamic law) can be a condition for purchase and distribution.

Cosmetics notification is easier to prepare for once you know how it is structured. Southeast Asian countries have aligned the framework of their rules through the ASEAN Cosmetic Directive, and they use a system of notifying the authorities before a product is manufactured, imported or sold. Taking Malaysia as an example, the party responsible for the notification must be a company registered in that country. A Korean company cannot file it directly, so you have to appoint a local representative, and you should decide in whose name the notification is filed together with the trade terms. Notifications have a validity period and must be renewed in time. If an efficacy claim reads like treatment or prevention, the product is classified as a drug, not a cosmetic, and the procedure becomes much heavier.

Halal carries different weight in each country. Indonesia has been making certification mandatory in stages, starting with food and beverages and extending to categories such as cosmetics, with a deadline for each category. Products containing non-halal ingredients such as pork or alcohol are not banned from sale. They must state that fact clearly on the packaging and be handled separately. In Malaysia, halal certification is not mandatory for cosmetics, but you need a certificate to put a halal mark on the product or use it in advertising. Check first when and how the rules apply to your category.

Some rules block a way of selling altogether. Indonesia has banned overseas sellers from selling goods below a certain value directly on online marketplaces. It has also barred sales that complete payment inside a social media app, requiring the social function and the transaction function to be separated. Products sold online are subject to the same national standards and food and drug authority requirements as products sold offline.

The regulatory points here are general information. Confirm how they apply to your case with the relevant authorities or a professional.

Ship directly from Korea, or hold stock locally?

There are two sales models. Shipping each order from Korea is the lighter way to start. There are routes for opening a store without a local company, and some platforms connect delivery from a collection warehouse in Korea to the destination country. A typical arrangement is that the seller packs each order and sends it to a designated collection point in Korea, and the platform's logistics service handles customs clearance and local delivery. This model usually comes with a deadline for the parcel to reach the collection point after the order, so stores that are slow to ship within Korea should check that deadline first. The trade-offs are long delivery times, returns that are hard to get back, and products that cannot be sold this way in some countries and categories.

Holding stock locally makes delivery faster and makes it easier to get good placement in events. But it is a formal import, so a local importer and product permits must be in place first, and you carry the risk of tied-up inventory.

That is why the usual order is to confirm which products sell through direct shipping and then move only those products to local stock. If you are weighing online sales against wholesale to buyers, see also the criteria in Exporting to China: B2B wholesale or cross-border e-commerce first?

What should you do in the first three months?

  1. Pick one country and one platform. Start by checking whether your product can be sold in that country by direct shipping.
  2. Cut the range to a few products. Light, unbreakable products with a long shelf life suit international shipping.
  3. Calculate what you keep per unit. From the selling price, subtract the commission, international shipping, payment fees, your share of discount coupons and return shipping costs. For example, if the selling price is 10,000 won, these costs add up to 6,000 won and the product cost is 4,000 won, you lose more with every event you join.
  4. Prepare product pages and reply templates in the local language. Many chat inquiries come in before purchase, so stores that answer quickly have the advantage.
  5. Join one regular sales event. Record sales, reviews and returns before and after the event, and choose which products to continue.

Common mistakes

  • Opening in several countries at once. Customer service and event calendars differ by country, so none of them gets proper attention.
  • Converting the Korean price by the exchange rate only. Set the price by looking at similar local products and shipping costs together.
  • Opening a video channel and not posting videos. On TikTok Shop, listing the product is the beginning, not the end. If nobody has been assigned to make videos, a search-based marketplace is the better choice.
  • Putting off permits until the product sells. If a product is taken down for being sold without notification, the reviews you built up disappear with it.
  • Not preparing for counterfeits and parallel imports. Online marketplaces are sometimes cited as a main channel for counterfeit goods. Check whether you have filed your trademark locally and whether you can prove your store is the official one.
