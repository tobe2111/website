Negotiating samples and MOQ (minimum order quantity, the smallest quantity you accept in one order) is not a question of whether to give a discount. It is a matter of trading one term for another. Samples are paid by default, with the amount deducted from the main order. For MOQ, you lower the quantity and adjust the unit price, packaging or payment terms in return. To do that, you first need to be able to explain why your MOQ is the number it is.

Key takeaways

  • MOQ is not a number you hold out on. It is a calculation that comes from production, materials, packaging and transport.
  • When you lower the quantity, change one of the following with it: unit price, packaging method or payment terms.
  • Decide who pays for the sample and the freight before you ship.
  • Put agreed terms in an email and a quote the same day.

How is MOQ determined?

MOQ comes from four places: the minimum volume from one run of the production line, the minimum unit in which materials such as containers and labels can be ordered, the quantity that fits in one carton, and transport units such as a pallet or a container. The largest of these constraints becomes your MOQ.

Once you know where the number comes from, you can see where it can be loosened. If the constraint is a custom-printed container, you can lower the quantity by putting a sticker label on an existing container. If the constraint is the production line, you can ship first from stock you have already made. With buyers too, "it is because of the minimum order for container printing" keeps the conversation going better than "it is our policy".

How do you answer when a buyer asks for a lower MOQ?

It is natural for a first-time buyer to want a small quantity. They cannot take on a large volume of a product that may or may not sell in their market. Do not refuse, and do not simply agree. Propose what you will give and what you will get as a pair.

Buyer's requestWhat you can giveWhat you get in return
A small quantity for the first order onlySet a separate trial order quantitySmall-quantity unit price applies, with the regular MOQ from the second order
A mix of several itemsCalculate MOQ on the combined total across itemsCarton units are kept for each item
Local-language packaging in a small quantityPut local-language stickers on existing packagingDedicated packaging only once the regular MOQ is met
Same unit price, smaller quantityShip right away from stock on handPayment in advance, and acceptance of the stock items and manufacturing dates as they are

Work out unit prices by quantity in advance. For example, for a product with a regular MOQ of 3,000 units and a unit price of USD 2, a 500-unit trial order gets a separate unit price that spreads material loss and handling costs over the smaller quantity. If you put both prices side by side on one quote, the buyer works out for themselves how much better off they are with a larger quantity. These numbers are a made-up example for illustration.

Should samples be sent for free?

Paid is the default. The reason to charge for samples is not profit. It is to confirm that the other side is serious. Inquiries that only collect samples mostly go quiet once cost comes up. In practice, the following approach causes the least friction.

  1. Charge for the sample and deduct it from the main order. The buyer loses nothing, and you create one more reason for the sample to lead to an order.
  2. The buyer pays the freight. Shipping on the buyer's own courier account is the cleanest way.
  3. Send documents with it. A sample becomes review material only when the product specification sheet, ingredient list, price list by quantity and carton dimensions go with it.
  4. Set the schedule after arrival in advance. Before you ship, agree on how many days after receipt the buyer will give feedback.

There are cases where sending for free is the better choice, such as a new product sample for a buyer you already trade with, or a large retailer you met at a trade show. Set the principle, then judge the exceptions. Food and cosmetics can be subject to the importing country's customs rules even as samples. Korea's own system gives a sense of this. Korea lets low-value samples for business use clear customs with only a list submission and no import declaration, but it excludes food, health functional foods, medicines, agricultural, livestock and fishery products subject to quarantine, and some cosmetics. Goods whose description or price is stated inaccurately are excluded as well. The importing country may draw similar lines. So even for a free sample, state the true product description and price on the invoice. Deliberately understating the price does not end with a customs delay. It can be subject to penalties. Rules differ by country and product and can change, so check the latest guidance from a customs broker or your carrier before you ship.

How do you record what was agreed?

Terms agreed by phone or messenger should be written up again and sent by email the same day. If quantity, unit price, trade terms, payment method, lead time, and conditions such as "trial order only" are missing, you will be asked for the same terms on the next order. Before a formal order, put the same content in a pro forma invoice (a quote prepared in the form of an invoice). It should include the names and addresses of the seller and the buyer, the buyer's reference number, the items, unit prices and totals, weights and dimensions, any discount, trade terms stated down to the delivery point, payment terms, estimated shipping date and the validity period of the quote.

This document is not an informal memo. The buyer uses the pro forma invoice to apply for an import license, arrange pre-shipment inspection, or open a letter of credit and start the remittance process. It may not be a document required at shipment, but it is the basis on which the buyer and the importing country's authorities know in advance what the incoming cargo will be. So the principle is that once it is sent, its content is not changed without the buyer's consent. This is why, if you quote a small-quantity unit price for a trial order, the validity period and "this order only" must be written in the same document. Common mistakes

  • Cutting MOQ in half without being able to explain the basis. You also lose trust in your original number.
  • Applying the regular unit price to a small order, and losing money on every unit sold.
  • Accepting a low quantity without the "trial order" condition, so that quantity becomes the baseline.
  • Sending a sample and then only waiting to hear back.
  • Sample specifications differing from the production goods, leading to a dispute on the first delivery.
