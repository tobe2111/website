Your first email to an overseas buyer should be short and easy to answer. One sentence on why you are writing to this person, two or three sentences on why your product fits their store, and one request to finish. A long email with the company history and the full catalog is closed before it is read.

Key takeaways

  • In a first email, a buyer looks at two things: "Was this sent to me?" and "Does this product fit my store?"
  • Put the product and the nature of your proposal in the subject line. A subject with only a greeting does not get opened.
  • Make one request only. Asking whether you may send samples is the easiest to answer.
  • If there is no reply, write again up to two or three times, adding one new piece of information each time.

What does a buyer look at in a first email?

Proposals from unknown suppliers pile up in a buyer's inbox, and the time spent on each one is not long. In that short time the buyer judges two things.

One is whether it was sent to them. An email with no recipient name, filled with sentences that would work for any company, looks like a mass mailing. Even one line showing that you looked at the buyer's store or product range changes how it reads.

The other is whether the product will sell in their store. The buyer is not looking at how impressive your company is. They are looking at whether this product will sell to their customers and leave a margin. So what is needed is evidence, not praise for yourself.

What goes into the first email?

PartWhat to writeLength
SubjectProduct, country of origin, nature of the proposal. Example: Korean rice snack for your Asian grocery lineOne line
Opening sentenceWhy you are writing to this buyer. A specific point of contact such as their store, their product range or a trade show where you metOne sentence
ProductWhat it is, who buys it, how it differs from similar productsTwo or three sentences
EvidenceCertifications you hold, export readiness (English labels, minimum order quantity), sales channels that can be verifiedTwo or three lines
RequestOne of two: agreement to receive samples, or confirmation of who the right contact isOne sentence

Write only facts in the evidence. If you blur a certification you do not hold by calling it "in progress", or inflate your sales record, it comes out during checks at the sample stage, and it is hard to talk to that buyer again. You do not need to attach a price list to the first email. Sending it with the trade terms after the buyer shows interest leads to fewer misunderstandings. What does it look like in practice?

Below is a made-up example to show the structure. Replace the text in square brackets to fit your own situation.

Subject: [Product] from Korea for [Buyer's store or category]

Hi [Name],

I saw that [Buyer's company] carries [related category], and I think our [product] could fit that shelf.

We are [Company], a Korean maker of [product]. It is [one clear difference], and it is made for [target customer].

We hold [certification], have English labels ready, and can start from [MOQ].

May I send you a few samples? If someone else handles this category, I would be grateful if you could point me to them.

Best regards,

[Name, title, company, website]

A good length for the body is one that can be read on a phone screen without scrolling. Send no attachment, or cut it down to a single one- or two-page introduction. People rarely open large files from someone they do not know. Put your company website address in the signature.

Even a well-written email goes unread if it lands in the spam folder. Gmail requires every sender who sends email to Gmail accounts to set up sender authentication for their domain (SPF or DKIM), and says that mail from domains with a high rate of spam reports from recipients may be marked as spam or blocked. Send from your company domain address. You can find out whether authentication is set up by asking your email service or domain provider. Sending one email at a time to selected buyers, instead of sending the same text to hundreds of addresses at once, helps deliverability as well as replies.

When and how do you follow up if there is no reply?

No reply usually does not mean no. It means the email has not been read yet or has been put aside. Write again at intervals, but do not repeat the same email.

  1. About a week later: send it as a reply in the same thread as the earlier email. Cut it to two or three sentences and ask again only whether you may send samples.
  2. One or two weeks after that: add one new piece of information. It should be something that gives the buyer a reason to look again, such as a newly obtained certification, the dates of a trade show you will attend or a new pack size.
  3. One last time: write that the timing does not seem right at the moment and that you will get in touch again later, and close.

If there is still no reply after that, stop, and contact them again next season or before a trade show. If they reply asking you to stop writing, remove them from your list right away.

Sending the same promotional email to many buyers can be treated as advertising, not as a business proposal. Korean law takes the recipient's prior consent as the principle for sending commercial advertising information by email, and requires the sender's name and contact details and a way to opt out to be stated in the email. Sending again to someone who has opted out, sending to automatically generated addresses and hiding the sender are prohibited. Many receiving countries have similar rules. This is general information. Confirm how it applies to your case with the relevant authorities or a professional. Detailed requirements can change, so before sending in bulk, check the latest guidance for the target country and your email service.

Common mistakes in a first email

  • Starting with "Dear Sir/Madam" and no recipient name. Find the name of the person in charge first.
  • Filling half the email with the company introduction. What the buyer wants to know is the product and the terms.
  • Putting every product into one email. Choose only the one or two that fit the buyer's store.
  • Sending machine-translated sentences without editing. Rewrite them as short, simple sentences.
  • Preparing samples, English materials and quotation terms only after a reply arrives. If your answer takes days, interest cools.
