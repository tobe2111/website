The best way to answer a negative review is quickly, with facts and actions rather than emotion. Far more people read a reply than the one person who wrote the review: they are the next customers deciding whether to visit. Follow four steps, thanks and empathy, the facts, a concrete fix and a way to talk, and you will rarely go wrong.

Key takeaways

  • The real audience for a reply is future customers. A calm, careful reply builds trust on its own.
  • Do not argue with an unhappy reviewer in public. Briefly state what you found and what you will change.
  • Never put personal details such as orders, bookings or treatment in a reply.
  • Avoid offering perks on condition that a review is deleted or edited.

Why do replies matter?

Customers comparing businesses on Naver Place read reviews and owner replies along with the star rating. A shop that replies warmly to praise and calmly to complaints comes across as one that takes responsibility when something goes wrong. Missing replies, or replies that argue with customers, can cost you more than the review itself.

In what order should you answer a complaint?

Take a review that says "the wait was far too long". You might reply: "We are sorry you had to wait so long. We checked, and bookings that day were bunched into one time slot, so we were slow to seat you. From this week we have spaced out bookings and tell guests the expected wait in advance. If there is anything else you would like to tell us, please call the shop." It has the facts, the change and a channel to talk, with no excuses.

What should you never do in a reply?

Clinics and consultation businesses in particular must not mention treatment or consultation details in replies. Even if the reviewer mentioned them first, repeating them as the business can become a personal data problem. For false or defamatory reviews, use the platform's reporting process rather than fighting back in a reply.

Reply checklist

  • Do you check new reviews at least daily and reply within a day or two where possible?
  • Is each reply tailored to the review, not a pasted sentence?
  • Did you include the facts and a concrete fix in replies to complaints?
  • Have you left out personal data and order, booking or treatment details?
  • Do you collect recurring complaints and turn them into real changes?
