Key takeaways

  • Open marketplaces make price comparison easy, which quickly sparks a race to the bottom.
  • A discount price that goes public becomes the "real price" in the minds of partners and shoppers.
  • Combining channels where prices are less visible protects both margin and partner trust.
  • This means giving up open marketplace search traffic, so it suits some products better than others.

Why do prices collapse on open marketplaces?

On open marketplaces such as Coupang or Naver Smart Store, where anyone can sign up and sell, many sellers can list the same product at once. Shoppers sort by price and pick the cheapest, and sellers keep shaving their prices to win the sale. Add coupons and promotions, and the price hits the floor fast.

The problem is that this price is visible to anyone with one search. Group buying sellers hear "it's cheaper online", and offline retailers lose any reason to hold their shelf price.

What does a brand lose when its price collapses?

  • Margin: sales volume may rise while profit shrinks.
  • Partner trust: the distribution partners who held the list price are the first to leave.
  • Brand image: once you are known as "always on sale", selling at full price gets hard.
  • Export leverage: overseas buyers sometimes check Korean online prices, which can weaken your position in supply price talks.
