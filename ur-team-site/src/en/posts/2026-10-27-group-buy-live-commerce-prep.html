Before you start a group buy or a live-commerce sale, settle three things first: the sale price and what happens to it afterwards, the stock you will prepare, and the payout terms. These channels sell a lot in a short time, so if any of the three is vague you can end up with large sales but little profit, or find it hard to sell at full price again.

Key takeaways

  • Before setting the group-buy price, draw a price ladder from list price to supply price to cost.
  • Agree when the price returns to list after the sale ends.
  • Selling out and overstocking both cost money. Fix the quantity and whether you can restock.
  • If an influencer is paid to promote the product, the ad or sponsorship must be clearly disclosed.

How do you set the price?

Set the group-buy price by how it fits with your other channels, not by how low it can go. Line up list price, group-buy or live price, the supply price you give the channel, and cost plus logistics, and you can see how far you can go. If the group-buy price is too far below list, shoppers anchor on it after the event, and selling at full price becomes difficult.

How much stock should you prepare?

There is no fixed answer, but it helps to set a range based on the host's past sales, audience size and sales of similar products. Sell out and you lose sales; prepare too much and clearing leftovers with discounts can break your price. Check in advance how quickly you can produce more and the minimum production run, and the decision gets easier.

What do you agree on payout and responsibility?

Put supply price and commission, payout cycle, who ships and by when, and who pays for exchanges and returns in writing. Agree up front on the sales data you will receive afterwards (units sold, return reasons and so on), as it is the basis for the next round. Paid promotional content needs an ad or sponsorship disclosure, so confirm how the host will show it.

Checklist before you start

  • Have you put list price, group-buy price, supply price and cost in one table?
  • Have you agreed the sale period and when list price returns?
  • Have you confirmed stock on hand and whether you can restock?
  • Are payout cycle, shipping and return costs set down in writing?
  • Have you confirmed how sponsorship will be disclosed?
  • Does the price clash with any other sales channel?
