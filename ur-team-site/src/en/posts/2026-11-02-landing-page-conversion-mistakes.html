If your ads get steady clicks but few inquiries or purchases, check the page people land on before you change the ad. A page that opens with different words from the ad, hides the inquiry button or loads slowly on mobile leaks money however good the ad is.

Key takeaways

  • If click-through rate (CTR) is fine but conversion rate (CVR) is low, suspect the landing page first.
  • Most ad clicks happen on phones, so check against the mobile first screen.
  • Match the first-screen headline to the ad copy and make the inquiry button visible without scrolling.
  • If you do not confirm that inquiries and purchases are recorded (conversion tracking), you cannot tell whether changes help.

Why check the landing page first?

An ad only brings people in. Getting them to stay and act is the page's job. In the numbers, this is the situation where CTR is normal and CVR is low. We explain how to read these metrics in how to read ad metrics: CTR, CVR and ROAS.

What are the seven common mistakes?

  1. A first screen that doesn't match the ad: someone who clicked "free first consultation" and lands on your company history leaves at once.
  2. A hidden inquiry button: if people must scroll a long way to find it, they leave before acting.
  3. A form that is too long: name and contact details are enough at first. Ask the rest during the consultation.
  4. Trust signals nobody can check: real photos, a location and certificates build more trust than calling yourself "the best".
  5. Slow loading: large images and videos delay the first screen on mobile. Start by shrinking image files.
  6. Broken mobile layout: pages checked only on desktop often have cut-off text or overlapping buttons on phones.
  7. No conversion tracking: if you cannot see which ad produced an inquiry, you cannot know what to fix.

How do you check?

The simplest way is to click your own ad. On a phone, with Wi-Fi off, imagine you are seeing it for the first time and check whether within five seconds you can tell what the business does and how to get in touch. Ask two or three people around you to do the same for a fairer test. Change one thing at a time and compare the numbers over one or two weeks, rather than changing several things at once, and you will know what worked.

Landing page checklist

  • Do the ad copy and the first-screen headline say the same thing?
  • Is the inquiry or purchase button visible without scrolling?
  • Does the form ask only for what is truly needed?
  • Is there verifiable proof, such as real photos, location or certification?
  • Does the page open fast on a phone without breaking?
  • Are inquiries and purchases recorded as conversions in your ad dashboard?
