If you are starting social media marketing in China, Xiaohongshu (RED) often fits when you want to build trust and search visibility first, and Douyin often fits when you want reach and sales in a short time. In practice many brands use both: they build reviews and information on Xiaohongshu, then expand with Douyin short videos and live streams once a product is ready to sell.

Key takeaways

  • Xiaohongshu is a community for lifestyle tips and product reviews, where Chinese consumers search before buying.
  • Douyin is a short-video platform where a recommendation feed decides reach, linked to live commerce and shopping features.
  • Official brand accounts and selling features have platform-specific eligibility rules, and foreign companies often need a local partner.
  • Both require you to follow local rules, such as labelling promotional content.

How do the two platforms differ?

Xiaohongshu is made up of photo-and-text "notes" and short videos, and users search topics such as cosmetics, travel, clinics and restaurants to find other people's experiences. Honest reviews and informative content stay searchable for a long time, which suits building awareness and trust steadily. On Douyin, videos spread through a recommendation feed, so you can reach many people quickly, and live streams and in-app shopping turn viewing into buying straight away. Price competition is fierce, though, so sales terms need planning in advance.

Where should you start for each goal?

If your brand is barely known in China, it is safer to build notes from people who have tried your product on Xiaohongshu first. A brand with nothing to show in search struggles to earn trust even with live-stream discounts. If a sales route is already open through cross-border or B2B, and your stock and price terms are ready, you can move on to growing sales with Douyin short videos and live streams.

What should you check before starting?

Verifying an official account, connecting shopping features and running ads each come with business eligibility and document requirements that differ by platform and change often. Much of it is hard for a foreign company to do alone, so working with a local partner is common. Checklist before you start

  • Have you decided whether this stage is about awareness or sales?
  • Have you prepared Chinese product names and key search terms that consumers might use?
  • Have you checked account eligibility and found a local partner?
  • If you are going to sell, are the sales route (cross-border or B2B), stock and price terms ready?
  • Have you checked local rules such as labelling promotional content?
