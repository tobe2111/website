The results of a blog review campaign, where bloggers receive your product or service and write about their experience, depend less on how many bloggers you recruit and more on which keywords, which bloggers and what they write. With four steps in place, namely keyword selection, blogger screening, a writing guide and follow-up review, you get reviews that stay in search longer for the same budget.

Key takeaways

  • About half of the traffic after a Naver search goes to blogs, so review posts carry real weight.
  • Decide the keywords customers actually search before you recruit anyone.
  • Whether a blog ranks well for those keywords matters more than its follower count.
  • Compensated reviews must disclose it, so include disclosure wording in your guide.

Why blog review campaigns?

According to the data on our blog marketing page, 49% of traffic after a Naver search goes to blogs. People look for real user reviews before buying, and they trust posts that are specific and knowledgeable. A review campaign is a way to produce several of these posts in a short time.

Why start with keywords?

However good a review is, it will not be read if it uses words customers never search. Start by thinking about what customers search for, and in what situation. For a health food, searches that carry a concern, such as "foods good for your tonsils" or "foods good for your airways", lead to purchases. For a gift product, it might be "gift for in-laws" or "Chuseok holiday gift".

Which bloggers should you choose?

  • Visibility: do their recent posts rank high for your chosen keywords?
  • Topic fit: does the blog's usual subject match your product?
  • Consistency: have they been posting regularly until recently?
  • Engagement: do comments and likes come from real followers?

What goes into the writing guide?

ItemContent
Required keywordsSearch terms to use in the title and body
Key messageWhich strengths, differences or usage tips must come across
PhotosRequired shots (packaging, in use, results) and how many
Expressions to avoidClaims that assert results, exaggerated comparisons
DisclosureWording and placement stating the product or payment was provided
ScheduleTrial period, publishing deadline, how to request edits

In tightly regulated fields such as healthcare, review campaign posts can be treated as advertising, so extra care is needed. Please also read our post on medical advertising rules.

What should you check afterward?

Do not stop at publication. For each post, record its keyword rank, the traffic it brought and whether it led to inquiries or sales. The keywords and blogger types that performed well become the standard for your next campaign. Strong reviews can also be reused on product pages or in ads, as long as you agree on the scope of use with the blogger in advance.
