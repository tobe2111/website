A unit price on an export quote only means something when it comes with an Incoterm, the international trade term. Ten dollars handed over at your factory (EXW) and ten dollars delivered to the buyer's warehouse with duties paid (DDP) put completely different costs and risks on the seller. For a first quote, it is safest to choose a term that keeps your responsibility within the stretch you can actually manage.

This article is a plain-language summary of the International Chamber of Commerce's Incoterms 2020. For a real contract, check the official rules and ask your freight forwarder and customs broker.

Key takeaways

  • Incoterms are international rules on who pays for transport, insurance and customs, and where risk passes.
  • EXW puts the least on the seller and DDP the most. FOB and CIF sit in between.
  • Under CIF the seller pays ocean freight and insurance, but risk passes to the buyer once goods are on board at the port of shipment.
  • Always name the place after the term, for example FOB Busan or CIF Long Beach.

How do the four terms differ?

  • EXW (Ex Works): the seller only makes the goods available at its factory or warehouse. Loading and export clearance are the buyer's job, so in practice many exporters use a term like FCA instead to avoid export declaration problems.
  • FOB (Free On Board): the seller clears export and is responsible until the goods are loaded on the vessel at the port of shipment. The buyer pays from ocean freight onwards.
  • CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight): the seller pays freight and cargo insurance to the destination port, but, as with FOB, risk passes at the port of shipment.
  • DDP (Delivered Duty Paid): the seller delivers to the buyer's named place and pays import clearance, duties and taxes. Without a partner to handle local customs, the risk is high.

Shipping by container? What to use instead of FOB

FOB and CIF were designed for sea and inland waterway transport. Container cargo is usually handed over at a terminal before it is loaded, so the ICC recommends FCA or CIP in those cases. Buyers still often ask for FOB quotes out of habit, so confirm the actual handover point with your forwarder to avoid disputes.

Which term suits a first quote?

If you are new to exporting, terms where your responsibility ends inside Korea, such as FOB or FCA, are easier to manage. If the buyer wants CIF or DDP, get freight, insurance, local duties and VAT, and clearance fees from your forwarder in advance and build them into the unit price. Leave them out and every sale can lose money. What buyers look for in a quote is also covered in what to prepare before meeting US B2B buyers.

Quote checklist

  • Did you write the term together with the named place (e.g. FOB Busan)?
  • Did you state the price currency and how long the quote is valid?
  • Did you include the minimum order quantity and production and shipping lead time?
  • Have you settled payment terms (deposit share, letter of credit, etc.)?
  • Are packing unit, carton size, weight and HS code ready?
  • For CIF or DDP, have you confirmed freight, insurance and local taxes with a forwarder quote?
