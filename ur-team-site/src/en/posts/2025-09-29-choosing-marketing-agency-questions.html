When choosing a marketing agency, listen first to how they answer three things: whose name the ad accounts are in, how costs are split, and what data results are reported with. An agency that answers these clearly usually works transparently elsewhere too. One that promises guaranteed results deserves a second look.

Key takeaways

  • Ad accounts, copy and data should stay in your company's name and ownership.
  • Check ad spend and the agency fee separately.
  • A promise of guaranteed rankings or sales is a warning sign, not a reason to trust.
  • Avoid agencies that suggest fake reviews or hiding paid posts. Legal responsibility can fall on the advertiser too.

What should you ask at the first meeting?

  1. Have you worked with businesses like ours? What was difficult?
  2. What numbers will we use as goals for the first three months?
  3. Whose name will the ad accounts be in, and what happens when the contract ends?
  4. How much is ad spend and how much is your fee, and how are they billed?
  5. How often do you report, and with what data? Can we see the ad dashboard ourselves?
  6. Who will actually handle our account, and how many other clients do they manage?
  7. If results are poor, what will you change, and when will you decide?
  8. Which laws and rules should our industry watch? (For clinics, medical advertising standards.)
  9. Who owns the copy, photos and videos you create?
  10. What are the contract term, cancellation conditions and penalties?

What should you look for in the answers?

How they answer matters more than the questions. Agencies that propose setting goals together and tell you the limits up front make better long-term partners. If they give only a total figure without a breakdown, say they cannot hand over accounts and materials, or lead with contract length and penalties, think again.

Why be careful with guarantees?

Search rankings and ad results depend on platform rules, competitors and market conditions. An honest agency promises what it will measure and how it will improve, not the outcome. Clinics in particular carry responsibility for their ad wording, so avoid agencies that write copy stating results as certain. Checklist before signing

  • Will the ad accounts be opened in your company's name?
  • Are ad spend and agency fee listed separately in the contract?
  • Are the reporting cycle and materials (including raw dashboard data) defined?
  • Does it state who owns copy, photos and video, and how they are handed over at the end?
  • Are cancellation terms and penalties reasonable?
