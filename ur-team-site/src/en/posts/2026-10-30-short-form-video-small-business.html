If you run short-form video yourself, building a routine that lets you post every week is more realistic than making one perfect video. Use the questions customers ask most as topics, shoot several videos in one quiet hour, and add captions with the app's built-in editor. You can keep it going without special equipment or editing skills.

Key takeaways

  • Find topics in what is already in your shop: common customer questions, how things are made, today's menu or new products.
  • Show what the video is about in the first two or three seconds, and keep each video to one thing.
  • Batch shooting for about 30 minutes once a week gives you several videos to post.
  • Choose the next topic by completion rate and saves or shares, not just views.

What should you film?

You do not need to invent new ideas. The best material is what repeats every day: questions customers ask at the counter ("How should I store this?"), hands at work, today's ingredients or new arrivals, and how to find your shop. If staff or customers appear, get their consent first, and use only music the platform allows.

How should one video be structured?

First, stop the thumb that is scrolling. Open with the finished result or a one-line question, and show just one thing in the middle. End with a single action for the viewer, such as your location, how to book, or "save this". Many people watch without sound, so always add captions.

How do you keep it up when you are busy?

Trying to shoot every day burns you out fast. Split the week into idea notes, a batch shoot, staggered posting and a review, and one shoot covers several posts. The example below is just one option; move the days to suit your shop.

Short-form checklist

  • Do you have a topic list built from customers' common questions?
  • Is it clear in the first two or three seconds what the video is about?
  • Does each video cover only one thing?
  • Did you add captions, and is the music cleared for use?
  • Did everyone who appears give consent?
  • Do you pick the next topic by completion rate and saves?
