Xiaohongshu is a social platform widely used in China for searching reviews and information. To make a hospital known there, build up posts that answer "what do I need to do, and how, to visit a hospital in Korea" instead of promoting procedures. Users do not come to look at ads. They search and compare reviews and information. Medical content has to pass the platform's review and follow the rules of both Korea and China, so the preparation has an order.

The content below is general information. Confirm how it applies to your case with the relevant authorities or a professional.

Key takeaways

  • Xiaohongshu is a place people use by searching. A post stays in search for a long time after it is published.
  • Before the account, put in place your registration for attracting foreign patients and a Chinese-language inquiry channel.
  • Choose topics that reduce anxiety before the visit: how to get there, the booking process, the flow of a visit and how costs are explained.
  • Do not use wording that asserts results or compares your hospital with others.

Why does Xiaohongshu suit hospitals?

The basic unit of Xiaohongshu is a set of photos and text called a "note". Before decisions that cost money and time, such as travel, shopping or a hospital visit, users type their question into the search bar and read the notes they find. They save the ones they like and take them out again when planning their schedule.

So organized information works harder here than video that scrolls past. Someone choosing a hospital abroad has a lot to check. They search one by one for how to get there, whether they will be understood and how to book. What you need is one post from your hospital for each of these questions. What should be in place before you create an account?

  1. Registration for attracting foreign patients. In Korea, a medical institution that wants to attract foreign patients must meet the requirements and register with its city or provincial government. The basic requirements are a specialist for each department that will receive foreign patients, and enrollment in medical malpractice liability insurance or a medical liability mutual aid association. The registration has a validity period and must be renewed before it expires. Requirements can change, so check the latest guidance before you proceed.
  2. Whether your partners are registered. A party that is not a medical institution but refers or brokers foreign patients must also register separately as a facilitator. Registered organizations can be looked up in the Foreign Patient Attraction Information System. In the annual performance report, a hospital also has to state the route by which each patient came, so patients who came through an overseas broker with unclear credentials stay on the record as such.
  3. An account in the hospital's name. Operating under the hospital's name requires the platform's organization verification. In the medical field the verification standards are strict and have changed over time. Start by checking which kinds of organizations can be verified now. Create the account in the hospital's name so that it stays with the hospital when an agency contract ends.
  4. A Chinese-language inquiry channel. Decide who will answer comments and direct messages in Chinese, and during which hours.
  5. Information materials in Chinese. Translate directions, how to book, how costs are explained and the cancellation policy in advance.
  6. Someone to check the wording. Appoint a person inside the hospital to read each post against medical advertising standards before it goes up.

For translation, do not carry over your Korean blog posts as they are. Rewrite them in the phrasing Chinese-speaking users search with.

What wording should you be careful with?

Even for posts aimed overseas, it is safer to stay within Korea's medical advertising principles. One point to know is that advertising "within Korea" to attract foreign patients is among the types of advertising the Korean Medical Service Act prohibits. The only exception, allowed after review, is advertising in a foreign language by a registered medical institution in limited places such as airports. Take care not to run Chinese-language content, just because you have made it, on outdoor media in Korea or in spaces seen by users in Korea.

China's advertising rules and the platform's own review come on top of this. The platform tends to look strictly at medical posts, so medical promotion from an account without verified credentials can lose reach or be removed.

  • Do not use wording that asserts or promises results.
  • Do not compare your hospital with others or run them down.
  • Do not use patients' treatment testimonials as a promotional tool.
  • If you have paid an influencer or a trial participant to post, have them disclose that fact.
  • When you state a price, state the conditions and what is included with it.

The platform's detailed standards can change, so check the latest guidance before you proceed.

Common mistakes

  • Answering inquiries late. Someone who reads a post and sends a message is asking several hospitals at the same time. An answer that comes days later is not read.
  • Putting contact details in comments first. The platform sometimes restricts steering users to outside contact channels. China's internet advertising rules also lean toward restricting posts that introduce health information from carrying a medical service provider's contact details or purchase links alongside. Check first what is allowed.
  • Filling the account with promotional prices. Inquiries that come for the price alone rarely lead to visits, and wording problems arise easily.
  • Setting follower count as the goal. What to count is saves, inquiries and actual bookings.
  • Posting a few times and stopping. This is a channel where posts that stay in search have to build up, so stopping takes you back to the beginning.
  • Promoting before you are ready to receive. If inquiries come in and nobody can answer in Chinese, the ad budget has simply been spent.
