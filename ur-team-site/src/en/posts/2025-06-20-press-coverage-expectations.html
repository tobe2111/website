Press coverage is less a way to lift sales with a single article and more a way to show credible information to people who search your brand and to back up sales and listing materials. Its value depends less on the day the story runs than on how consistently you reuse it on your website, product pages and proposals.

Key takeaways

  • Articles add credibility to brand search results and sales materials.
  • One article does not by itself raise sales or guarantee the outlet, placement or duration you want.
  • Paid advertorials must be marked so readers can tell they are ads.
  • Clinics need extra care, because Korean medical law restricts ads presented as news or expert opinion.

What can press coverage do for you?

Buyers, investors, retail listing managers and first-time customers search a company's name before doing business. Finding articles about your company gives the impression of a real, established business. It also leaves a public record of fact-based news such as product launches, export contracts and certifications.

What should you not expect?

It is rare for inquiries to flood in on the day an article runs. Articles cannot be targeted by age or region the way ads can, and placement and duration depend on the outlet's editorial decisions. Untrue articles or ones that inflate results damage trust and can cause problems. Paid advertorials must be labelled as advertising so readers do not confuse them with news.

Clinics should be even more careful. Korean medical law prohibits advertising presented as news articles or expert opinion in newspapers, broadcasts, magazines and similar media. See medical advertising rules to check before your clinic runs an ad for more.

What do you do after publication?

Link the article, with its source, in a press section of your website and on product pages, and attach it to buyer and listing proposals. Link it from social bios and your company profile too, and one article keeps working for a long time. When quoting an article, include the outlet name and date.

Press coverage checklist

  • Do you have a newsworthy fact, such as a launch, contract, certification or award?
  • Are the figures and claims in the article based on fact?
  • If you paid for the article, is it marked as advertising?
  • For clinics, have you checked that an article-style ad does not breach medical law?
  • Do you have a plan to reuse the article on your website, product pages and proposals?
