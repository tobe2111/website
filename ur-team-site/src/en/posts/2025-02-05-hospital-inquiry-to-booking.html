If inquiries are coming in but appointments are not growing, patients are more likely dropping out during follow-up than because of your ads. It typically happens when first replies are slow, after-hours inquiries are left waiting, staff give different answers, or there is no follow-up after booking. Check where the leaks are, step by step, and you will find improvements that do not need a bigger ad budget.

This article covers general operating practice and is not legal advice. For questions under Korean medical law, consult a professional.

Key takeaways

  • Patients move through search, inquiry, consultation, appointment and visit, and can drop out at each step.
  • Start by measuring first-response time on each channel: phone, Naver chat, Naver booking and KakaoTalk.
  • Prepare standard answers to common questions, without stating diagnoses or promising results.
  • Booking confirmations and day-before reminders reduce missed appointments from simple forgetting.

Where do patients drop off?

The most common leak is right after the inquiry. If staff cannot pick up during treatment, or an evening chat message gets a reply the next afternoon, the patient has already contacted another clinic. An after-hours auto-reply saying when you will respond, and calling first thing the next morning, already makes a difference.

What should you prepare for consultations?

Many consultations start with "How much is it?" and end with the price. Quote non-covered treatment fees exactly as your clinic has posted them, and explain why treatment plans differ by person and what is checked at the first visit, and the conversation continues. Avoid diagnosing or promising results over the phone or online. Drawing in patients with excessive discounts or gifts can also be a problem under Korean medical law. For wording standards, see medical advertising rules to check before your clinic runs an ad.

What happens after booking?

When an appointment is confirmed, send the date, time, location and anything to bring right away, then remind the patient the day before. Make it easy to reschedule and fewer people will simply not show up. Collect contact details and other personal data only with consent, and keep them only as long as needed.

Follow-up checklist

  • Do you record first-response time for each channel?
  • Does an auto-reply go out for inquiries outside clinic hours?
  • Do all staff use the same standard answers to common questions?
  • Does price guidance match the fees your clinic has posted?
  • Do you avoid language that states a diagnosis or promises results?
  • Do you send confirmations and reminders, and call back missed inquiries?
