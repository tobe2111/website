Before a clinic in Korea publishes an ad, it should check two things: that the ad contains no expressions banned under the Medical Service Act, and whether the medium requires prior review. Eye-catching expressions are often the riskiest, so the safest habit is to review every ad before it goes live.

This article is a general summary of common cautions in medical advertising and is not legal advice. For decisions on specific wording or cases, please check with a medical advertising review body or a legal professional.

Key takeaways

  • Korea's Medical Service Act sets out specific types of prohibited medical advertising.
  • Guaranteed results, comparisons with other clinics and use of patient testimonials are common problem areas.
  • Depending on the medium, an ad may need prior review before it runs.
  • Blogs, review campaigns and review events can count as ads too, so check them by the same standard.

Why is medical advertising regulated separately?

Patients find it hard to judge the quality of medical care in advance, and wrong information directly affects their health. That is why the Medical Service Act defines prohibited types, such as false or exaggerated ads and ads that mislead consumers. Even if an agency runs the ads, remember that the clinic is the advertiser.

Which expressions commonly cause problems?

  • Guaranteed results: promises such as "100% cure" or "no side effects"
  • Comparison or disparagement: claiming to be better than other clinics or other treatments
  • Treatment testimonials: using patient reviews in ads in a way that could mislead about results
  • Unsupported superlatives: words such as "best", "only" or "No. 1" without objective evidence
  • Before and after photos: these can be a problem because they may mislead
  • Missing key information: leaving out side effects or precautions
  • Excessive discounts and events: offering discounts or gifts in a way that may count as inducing patients

When is prior review required?

Medical ads must be reviewed in advance when they run in media defined by law, such as newspapers, magazines, outdoor advertising and internet media above a certain size. Reviews are carried out by medical advertising review bodies set up within the associations of doctors, dentists and Korean medicine doctors.

Which media require review, and how the review number must be displayed, can change with the law and the review bodies' guidance. Check the latest standards while you are still planning the ad.

What about blogs, review campaigns and reviews?

Posts that a clinic paid for or rewarded can be treated as advertising. Review campaign posts, review events and blog copy should all be checked against the prohibited types above, and any post written in return for compensation must clearly say so.
