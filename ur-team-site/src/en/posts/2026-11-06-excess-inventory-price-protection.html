To protect your brand's price, look first at B2B bulk buyouts, closed malls and overseas channels, where the sale price is less visible, rather than deep public discounts for slow-moving or short-dated stock. The key is to set a floor first: a price below which you never sell publicly.

Key takeaways

  • Public discounts are fast, but shoppers remember the price, which makes selling at list price harder later.
  • Put quantity, expiry, storage condition and cost in one sheet and your options become clear.
  • The shorter the remaining shelf life, the more you should favor fast channels where the price stays out of view.
  • Find out why the stock built up and adjust production and orders, or it will happen again.

Why not start with public discounts?

A deep discount on an open marketplace or a price-comparison site moves stock quickly, but the price stays in search results and in shoppers' minds. Customers who later pay full price feel cheated, and other retailers ask you to match. Clearing a few boxes ends up lowering the price of the whole brand. Which channels can you use?

Comparing options by clearance speed and price visibility makes the choice easier. A B2B bulk buyout moves everything at once without a public price. Closed and employee-welfare malls show prices only to members, so there is little clash with list price. Overseas channels keep the price separate from Korea but take time to prepare. Repacking stock as bundles or special sets lets you price it like a new product.

In what order should you proceed?

First, list quantity, expiry, storage condition and cost for each item in one sheet. Next, set your public price floor. The shorter the shelf life, the more you should choose fast, quiet channels first; if there is enough time left, consider overseas channels or repacking too. Finally, find out why the stock built up and adjust the next production run and orders. Donating or disposing of stock can change the tax treatment, so check with a tax professional.

Stock clearance checklist

  • Have you listed quantity, expiry, storage condition and cost for each item?
  • Have you set a public price floor?
  • Does the contract stop the buyer from reselling the stock on public channels?
  • Are expiry labels and storage conditions respected when you hand it over?
  • Have you checked the tax treatment for donation or disposal?
  • Have you found why the stock built up and reflected it in the next order?
