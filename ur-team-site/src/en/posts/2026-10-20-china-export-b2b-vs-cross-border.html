If you are looking at China for the first time, a sensible order for most brands is to approach a trustworthy local importer for B2B wholesale first, and to plan cross-border e-commerce alongside it for small tests and brand awareness. The two routes differ in paperwork, volume and price control, so the real question is not which one is better but which order suits your product and cash position.

Key takeaways

  • General trade (B2B wholesale) means a local importer buys in bulk. You need to meet import requirements for your category, but volume is steadier.
  • Cross-border e-commerce means Chinese consumers buy as overseas purchases. Separate rules apply, and small test batches are possible.
  • Cross-border listings rarely sell without local social content, and online prices are public, so prices need managing.
  • Regulations and platform policies change often. Check the current rules for your product before you start.

How does general-trade B2B wholesale work?

A Korean brand supplies a Chinese importer or distributor in bulk, and that partner handles distribution in China. Depending on the category, such as cosmetics or food, there may be registration or filing before import, Chinese labels or overseas manufacturer registration, so preparation takes time. Once a deal opens, though, orders are large and the brand does not carry local stock and promotion on its own.

What is different about cross-border e-commerce?

With cross-border e-commerce, Chinese consumers buy overseas products online directly. Different customs and tax rules apply, along with limits such as personal purchase caps, and for some categories entry is lighter than general trade. It is a good way to start small and see which products and price points get a response. But a product page alone will not sell. Content that creates demand, such as Xiaohongshu reviews, Douyin short videos and live streams, has to move with it.

Which order is safest?

There is no single answer, but a common way to lower risk looks like this. First confirm which import requirements apply to your product, then agree supply price and volume with a vetted B2B partner. In parallel, run a small cross-border test, and grow demand with content and live selling for the products that respond.

How do you protect your price?

If your B2B supply price, cross-border retail price and Korean price clash, local partners hesitate and even your home price can slip. Set a floor price per channel first, and agree the length and depth of any promotion in advance. Checklist before you start

  • Have you checked China's current registration, filing and labelling rules for your product?
  • Have you put B2B supply price, cross-border price and Korean price side by side?
  • Have you set the test volume and how much loss you can absorb if it fails?
  • Have you decided who makes local content (reviews, short video, live) and with what budget?
  • Have you filed your trademark in China in advance?
