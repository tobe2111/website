Your rank in Naver Power Link ads is decided by two things together: the bid you set for each keyword and the Quality Score Naver gives your ad. Raising the bid alone only raises your costs. Managing Quality Score as well is how you win a better position on the same budget.

Key takeaways

  • Power Link bids can be set per keyword, from a minimum of KRW 70 to a maximum of KRW 100,000.
  • Impressions are free. You pay only when someone clicks (CPC, cost per click).
  • When maximum bids are equal, the ad with the higher Quality Score ranks higher.
  • Quality Score comes from how well your keyword, ad copy and landing page fit together.

How is Power Link ad spend calculated?

Power Link shows an ad with your site name, description and URL when a user searches for a specific keyword. Simply appearing on screen costs nothing. You are charged each time someone clicks.

For example, if you bid KRW 100 on the keyword "study in Canada" and get 100 clicks, your ad spend is KRW 10,000. Your bid sets the most you are willing to pay for a single click. The actual cost per click is set by the competition and never goes above your maximum bid.

What is Quality Score, and why does it matter?

Quality Score is Naver's rating of your ad's quality. The ad manager shows it as a bar with seven levels. Newly added keywords start in the middle and move up or down based on performance.

It matters for a simple reason. With equal bids, the ad with the higher Quality Score appears higher, which means you can hold the same position for less money. With a low Quality Score, you have to keep raising your bid just to stay where you are.

What should you check to improve Quality Score?

ItemWhat to check
Ad group structureAre keywords with different intents mixed in one group?
Ad copyDoes the searched keyword appear naturally in the title and description?
Landing pageDoes the click go to a page that matches the search, not just your home page?
Ad extensionsHave you filled in phone number, location and extra links?
Click-through rateAre you keeping keywords switched on that almost never get clicks?

How should you set your bids?

  • Do not aim for first place by default. Judge by the cost of each inquiry or sale, not by rank.
  • Use specific keywords too. Keywords like "area + business type" or "treatment + area" face less competition and can rank high on lower bids.
  • Split by time and device. If inquiries peak at certain hours or days, or PC and mobile perform differently, adjust bids separately.
  • Review weekly. Daily numbers swing a lot, so decide based on at least a week of data.
