If you are targeting consumers in Korea, most businesses start with Naver search ads, and add Google search ads when they have overseas customers or an audience that relies heavily on Google. The right answer still depends on your industry and customers, so start by checking where your customers actually search.

Key takeaways

  • Both show ads to people who search and charge when someone clicks.
  • Naver is Korea's leading search platform, bringing together 54 million people a day.
  • Google reaches overseas customers and a wide range of placements such as YouTube and Gmail.
  • When running both, use one conversion definition so you can compare them fairly.

How do the two differ?

AspectNaver search adsGoogle search ads
Main usersMainly users in KoreaUsers in Korea and abroad, overseas markets

When is it better to start with Naver?

  • Shops, clinics and everyday services whose customers are mostly in Korea
  • Businesses that benefit from appearing alongside other Naver content, such as Place listings, blogs and cafe reviews
  • Products that many customers compare in Naver Shopping search

When should Google come first, or run alongside?

  • Services for overseas customers or foreigners, and B2B companies looking for export buyers
  • Audiences that rely heavily on Google, such as IT and developer tools
  • When you want to reach YouTube, Gmail and display placements in one go

On Google, UrTeam uses Search Ads to capture conversions from high-intent searches and PMAX to reach new customers, running both together.

What should you watch when running both?

  • One conversion definition: decide whether a purchase, booking or consultation request counts as a conversion, and use the same rule on both platforms.
  • Separate budgets: keep a budget per platform so you can tell which one produced results.
  • Avoid overlap: within Google, add your key keywords as negative keywords in PMAX so they do not compete with Search Ads.
  • Landing pages: whatever the platform, send clicks to a page that matches the search intent.
