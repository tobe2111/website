For a Naver blog post to keep getting read from search, what matters is not how many times you repeat the keyword but whether the structure fits what the searcher wants to know. Decide the search intent first, answer it straight away in the first paragraph, and turn the reader's next questions into subheadings.

Key takeaways

  • Search intent can be grouped into four broad types: informational, comparison, local and action.
  • A post that focuses on one intent is more likely to be read to the end.
  • Answer in the first paragraph and write subheadings as the questions readers ask next.
  • Your own photos and real experience build trust. Keyword stuffing and copied text can work against you.

What is search intent?

People who type the same words often want different answers. Someone searching "skin care" might want a method, a nearby shop or a price comparison. Before you write, type the keyword into the search box yourself and look at what kind of posts rank at the top. That tells you the main intent behind it.

How does the post change with each intent?

  • Informational: explain concepts and methods step by step. Diagrams or ordered lists help.
  • Comparison: set the criteria first, then compare in a table. Mention your own product's limits so it reads as fair.
  • Local: make location, opening hours, parking and directions clear, and keep them identical to your Naver Place listing.
  • Action: show the pricing basis and how to book or ask quickly. Do not hold up a reader who is ready to decide.

In what order should one post be written?

Answer the searched question in two or three sentences in the first paragraph. Then set the reader's likely follow-up questions as subheadings and answer them one by one. Add your own photos of the shop or process in the middle, and close naturally with the next step, such as booking, an inquiry or directions. Question subheadings and an answer up front also help when AI search summarizes the page.

Checklist before publishing

  • Can you state in one sentence which search intent this post answers?
  • Can a reader get the answer from the first paragraph alone?
  • Are the subheadings written as the reader's questions?
  • Does it include your own photos and real experience?
  • Have you avoided forcing the same keyword again and again?
  • If the post was paid for, have you disclosed it?
