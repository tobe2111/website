CTR shows whether your ad catches attention, CVR shows whether visitors go on to inquire or buy, and ROAS shows how much revenue your ad spend brought back. Read the three together and you can see which stage of your ad is stuck.

Key takeaways

  • CTR (click-through rate) = clicks ÷ impressions. It tells you whether your copy and keywords match.
  • CVR (conversion rate) = conversions ÷ clicks. It tells you whether your landing page and offer persuade.
  • ROAS (return on ad spend) = ad revenue ÷ ad spend. It tells you how much of your spend came back as revenue.
  • A high ROAS can still lose money if your margin is thin. Work out your break-even ROAS first.

What does each metric mean?

MetricFormulaWhat it tells you
CTR (click-through rate)Clicks ÷ impressions × 100What share of people who saw the ad clicked it
CVR (conversion rate)Conversions ÷ clicks × 100What share of visitors inquired or bought
ROAS (return on ad spend)Ad revenue ÷ ad spend × 100How much revenue each unit of ad spend produced
CPC (cost per click)Ad spend ÷ clicksThe cost of bringing in one visitor
CPA (cost per acquisition)Ad spend ÷ conversionsThe cost of one inquiry or sale

A conversion is whatever action the advertiser sets as the goal. For an online store it is a purchase, for a clinic a booking, and for a service business a consultation request.

Shall we try an example?

The numbers below are hypothetical, just to illustrate. Over one month, an ad was shown 10,000 times, clicked 200 times, and led to 6 purchases. Ad spend was KRW 400,000 and revenue from the ad was KRW 1,200,000.

  • CTR = 200 ÷ 10,000 = 2%
  • CVR = 6 ÷ 200 = 3%
  • CPC = KRW 400,000 ÷ 200 = KRW 2,000
  • CPA = KRW 400,000 ÷ 6 = about KRW 66,700
  • ROAS = KRW 1,200,000 ÷ KRW 400,000 = 300%

What should you fix when a metric is low?

SituationLikely causeWhat to try
Low CTRAd copy, keyword choiceRewrite copy to match search intent and switch off irrelevant keywords
High CTR but low CVRLanding page, price, trust signalsSend clicks to a page that matches the ad, and make reviews and the inquiry button easy to see
Decent CVR but low ROASCost per click, order valueTrim expensive keywords and raise order value with bundles

Is a high ROAS always a good ad?

Not necessarily. ROAS is based on revenue, so it ignores cost of goods and margin. To make sure ads are not losing you money, work out break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ margin rate first.

For example, if your margin after product cost, shipping and similar costs is 25%, your break-even ROAS is 400%. A ROAS of 300% may look good, but in that case every extra ad loses money. Ads shown to people searching your brand name also tend to post high ROAS because many of them were going to buy anyway, so evaluate them separately from ads that bring in new customers.
